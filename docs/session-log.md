2026-10-12, 16:29, [BUGFIX] Frame cost grew with distance explored: 110 -> 55 -> 30-40 fps over a long walk, never recovering. 2026-10-13, 16:25, [BUGFIX] Minimap player marker grew as the player looked up or down, eventually covering half the disc. Cause: the shader sizes the facing tick by measuring a fragment's perpendicular distance from the heading vector and its distance along it, and both of those tests are only meaningful for a UNIT vector - hand them one of length L and the tick comes out L times too wide and L times too long. It was being handed player.front's raw x and z, and front is a normalised *3D* direction whose horizontal part is cos(pitch): level 1.0, 60 degrees 0.5, 89 degrees 0.0175. So the marker's size was 1/cos(pitch), and at full upward gaze the perpendicular tolerance alone was 0.69 against a disc of radius 0.5 - the tick was wider than the map. That is exactly the reported "thickens, then swallows half the map", and it explains why it appeared to worsen the longer you looked: pitch does not return to level on its own. Fixed by normalising the horizontal component and dropping pitch entirely, which is also the correct reading of a heading on a plan view - a top-down map has no vertical component to show. The degenerate case needs state to handle honestly: looking straight up or down has no horizontal direction at all, so Minimap keeps the last heading that had one and the marker holds still instead of spinning or reporting a vector of zeros (a virgin map with no prior heading falls back to north, which is a guess the map admits to by having no better answer). Regression test sweeps all 179 reachable pitches and asserts the heading stays unit length, plus the hold-still and virgin-fallback cases. My first attempt at that sweep failed and the code was right: I seeded worstLength to 0f, so its deviation of 1 outranked every real reading and the "worst" it reported was my own sentinel. 2026-10-17, 23:35, [FEATURE] The light model, before any of it lights anything: Block Types declare `emission` (what they put into their own Cell) and `attenuation` (what they take away from light passing through, on top of the one level every step costs), and each Cell carries a packed light byte — Torchlight low nibble, Daylight high. LEAVES gets attenuation 1, so a forest floor is dimmer than a clearing while still being see-through; everything else emits nothing yet.

`attenuation` is deliberately only consulted for Block Types that are not opaque, because an opaque Block Type blocks light outright and has nothing on the far side to attenuate. That is a coupling to `opaque` and it is a choice, not an oversight: every opaque Block Type in this world is a solid the sun cannot reach through, and a separate property to say so independently would be a field nothing could yet disagree about.

The two levels are packed into one byte per Cell rather than kept as two arrays, because they are always read and written together and a Chunk holding a second 4096-byte array holds twice the memory for the same information. Every access goes through `Light.day`/`Light.torch`/`Light.pack` and `Chunk.packedLightAt` rather than touching the raw byte — the alternative is a `BlockType.getById` that silently answers AIR for an id with light in its high bits, and that failure is indistinguishable from missing terrain. `setBlock` clears the Cell's light, on the same reasoning that already invalidates the Surface cache: the numbers on either side of the change describe a world that no longer exists. Whether the levels *around* it are right is the engine's business.

Not yet wired to anything: nothing consumes this light and no Block Type emits. Committed separately so that the storage seam stands on its own, and so the next commit's diff is the algorithm rather than the scaffolding.

2026-10-18, 00:20, [FEATURE] Light is now visible: per-vertex light and corner occlusion, carried through an 11-float vertex, attributes 3 and 4, and two multiplies in the fragment shader. NOT VERIFIED BY EYE — everything below is the part that can be checked without a window, and the look is entirely for the playtest.

The vertex format went from eight floats to eleven: + aLight (vec2, Daylight then Torchlight, already divided by 15) and + aShade (corner occlusion 0..3). Light and shade are separate attributes rather than one packed float because light adds and shade multiplies, and folding them together meant unpicking them in the shader. Shade is per-vertex rather than per-face because a face is two triangles and interpolating between them is what the hardware already does; that interpolation is the entire mechanism that makes a corner darker than the middle of a wall.

Three things in Geometry.upload that are easy to get wrong and would not show up as errors:
- Attributes 3 and 4 are EXPLICITLY disabled for the shapes that do not carry them (the GUI quad, the selection box, the dropped-item cube). A disabled attribute reads the generic current value, which is global state and not part of the VAO — so a GUI quad would silently pick up whatever light happened to be current, and "happened to be current" is not something anyone should have to reason about.
- The fragment shader must take the `isGui` path before looking at Light or Shade, because a zero from a disabled attribute is not "no light", it is a real level of zero. Reading it as light would black out every screen-space quad in the game.
- `isEntity` was only ever declared in the vertex shader. The fragment shader now needs it to pick `lightLevel` over the vertex light, and glslangValidator caught it as undeclared rather than compiling to a shader that silently ignores entities' light.

Dropped items have no per-vertex light — they use the plain eight-float cube — so their light arrives through a `lightLevel` uniform set per Entity from the Cell they are lying in. Passed in as a lambda rather than reached for, on the same arrangement as SolidWorld: the caller supplies the one fact needed and the callee stays ignorant of where it came from.

Ten new checks, all of them about the data rather than the picture:
- Every vertex's light is inside 0..1 and its shade inside 0..3. Out-of-range would be silently clamped by the shader with no complaint.
- The Cells above a block read exactly 1.0, and the block's own underside is the darkest thing in its mesh at exactly 0.9833334. That number is (14 + 15 + 15 + 15) / 4 / 15, and the 14 is the interesting part: the block shades its own column, but light falls AROUND a lone block and back into the space beneath it, so the engine settles that Cell at 14 rather than 0. Asserting it exactly, rather than "less than 1.0", is deliberate — "less than 1.0" passes for a fully flat mesh, and a pitch-black underside would also pass, which is the same bug with the opposite sign.
- Vertices sharing a position within one face agree exactly, so a quad cannot be drawn with a crease down its diagonal. Compared within a face only: two different faces meeting at a corner sit at the same position ON PURPOSE and must shade differently, and that difference is what darkens the corner.
- A block tucked into a corner of a solid mass does occlude some of its own corners.
- A sealed room's interior faces carry Torchlight from the glowstone inside it and no Daylight at all.

Three of my own assertions were wrong again, all in the same direction as every earlier wrong turn in this feature: I assumed the Cell under a block is 0 when light falls around it to 14; I filtered vertices by y alone and caught the side faces' bottom rows, which read 1.0; and I compared same-position vertices across different faces, which legitimately differ. Six or seven wrong turns across this feature now, every one of them a test describing what I assumed rather than what must be true.
