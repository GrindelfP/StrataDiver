2026-10-13, 16:25, [BUGFIX] Minimap player marker grew as the player looked up or down, eventually covering half the disc. Cause: the shader sizes the facing tick by measuring a fragment's perpendicular distance from the heading vector and its distance along it, and both of those tests are only meaningful for a UNIT vector - hand them one of length L and the tick comes out L times too wide and L times too long. It was being handed player.front's raw x and z, and front is a normalised *3D* direction whose horizontal part is cos(pitch): level 1.0, 60 degrees 0.5, 89 degrees 0.0175. So the marker's size was 1/cos(pitch), and at full upward gaze the perpendicular tolerance alone was 0.69 against a disc of radius 0.5 - the tick was wider than the map. That is exactly the reported "thickens, then swallows half the map", and it explains why it appeared to worsen the longer you looked: pitch does not return to level on its own. Fixed by normalising the horizontal component and dropping pitch entirely, which is also the correct reading of a heading on a plan view - a top-down map has no vertical component to show. The degenerate case needs state to handle honestly: looking straight up or down has no horizontal direction at all, so Minimap keeps the last heading that had one and the marker holds still instead of spinning or reporting a vector of zeros (a virgin map with no prior heading falls back to north, which is a guess the map admits to by having no better answer). Regression test sweeps all 179 reachable pitches and asserts the heading stays unit length, plus the hold-still and virgin-fallback cases. My first attempt at that sweep failed and the code was right: I seeded worstLength to 0f, so its deviation of 1 outranked every real reading and the "worst" it reported was my own sentinel. LEAVES gets attenuation 1, so a forest floor is dimmer than a clearing while still being see-through; everything else emits nothing yet.

`attenuation` is deliberately only consulted for Block Types that are not opaque, because an opaque Block Type blocks light outright and has nothing on the far side to attenuate. That is a coupling to `opaque` and it is a choice, not an oversight: every opaque Block Type in this world is a solid the sun cannot reach through, and a separate property to say so independently would be a field nothing could yet disagree about.

The two levels are packed into one byte per Cell rather than kept as two arrays, because they are always read and written together and a Chunk holding a second 4096-byte array holds twice the memory for the same information. Every access goes through `Light.day`/`Light.torch`/`Light.pack` and `Chunk.packedLightAt` rather than touching the raw byte — the alternative is a `BlockType.getById` that silently answers AIR for an id with light in its high bits, and that failure is indistinguishable from missing terrain. `setBlock` clears the Cell's light, on the same reasoning that already invalidates the Surface cache: the numbers on either side of the change describe a world that no longer exists. 2026-10-18, 22:50, [FEATURE] The world is path traced. Not "ray traced lighting" — the rasterizer is gone from the default path and every pixel is now the average of rays finding their way through the Cells. The reason is written in the LightEngine's own history: light wrapping sideways under overhangs, a shaft lighting the room it pierced, leaves shadowing by accident. Each was a shortcut through the physics of light, and each shipped. There is no shortcut left here to get wrong — a shadow is a ray that did not arrive, and colour bleeding onto a wall from the grass beside it is the first bounce rather than a feature. SUN and MOON are sampled as area lights (a 2-degree disc, four times the real Sun's half-degree, because a faithful penumbra needs thousands of samples to stop looking like static); the sky is a direction-dependent model sampled by next-event estimation; MIS splits the sky between direct lighting and escaping bounces, and because both strategies sample the same cosine-weighted distribution the power-heuristic weight is exactly 0.5 everywhere — a coincidence that would silently stop being true the day either distribution changed, so it is written down where it lives. Cutouts are honoured by the ray: the art is sampled where the ray entered, so a hole in a leaf is a hole in its shadow, re-decided per ray. Dropped items are ray-traced as oriented boxes and participate in bounces, so they cast shadows and receive light; the selection wireframe is traced too, as an overlay that scatters nothing, so there is no depth buffer behind the picture for anything to sort against. Progressive: one sample per pixel per frame, discarded when the eye moves or the world changes (`ChunkStreamer.version`, which counts only the streamer's own Chunks — counting the Minimap's would reset the average a dozen times a second and the image would never get past one sample). Resolution adapts to a *measured* cost, never a guessed one.

Three real bugs, and the first is the one worth remembering: SHADOW RAYS STARTED EXACTLY ON THE SURFACE THEY WERE SHADING. A hit lands on a Cell boundary and rounding decides which side of it the coordinates fall on, so a ray leaving the hit point had a good chance of starting *inside* the solid — and every surface in the world reported itself occluded. The Sun was blocked by the floor it was shining on. It compiled, it ran, no error anywhere, and it looks like a world lit only by its own reflections, which is a perfectly plausible picture of a very wrong one. Fixed by stepping off the surface along its own normal before anything samples light from there, and by saying so at the point where it is done. The second: the aerial perspective applied its (1-transmittance) term twice, because the air's own contribution was both baked into the haze and multiplied by the mix — so the world's distance haze was quadratically weak and, since the haze was a constant colour, a sealed room with no light in it glowed faintly blue. The numbers matched 0.682 * fog^2 exactly, which is how it was identified. Now the in-scattered radiance is the sky's radiance in the direction being looked in, gated on the Daylight the hit Cell can see — the air scatters the sky's light, so there is none to scatter where the sky does not reach — and a sealed room reads exactly 0.0. The third: `Basis` divides by zero when the camera looks straight up or down, because forward and up are parallel. The player cannot quite do it (pitch clamps at 89 degrees) so this was unreachable in the game and reachable in a check.
